\paragraph{Practitioner points.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Under an equal CPU budget, splitting this application into services lowered peak throughput to
\xo{peakpct/max}\% or less of the monolith's. Allocating the CPU in proportion to each service's demand raised it to
at most \xo{prop/peakpct/max}\%
of the monolith's, and decomposition did not pay off at any load.
\item On one host, each synchronous call between services cost \xo{perhop/browse/net/min}--\xo{perhop/browse/net/max}\,ms
of transit at low load (\xo{perhop/browse/nethigh/min}--\xo{perhop/browse/nethigh/max}\,ms at moderate load) and a few
tenths of a millisecond of CPU,
and a sidecar mesh roughly doubled the transit cost; measure this per-hop price before deciding how finely to split.
\item In decomposed deployments the edge service's CPU quota set the capacity, and CFS throttling made tail latency
erratic before average utilisation reached the limit; watch the edge service's throttled-period counter.
\end{itemize}
