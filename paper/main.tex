\paragraph{Practitioner points.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Under an equal CPU budget, splitting this application into services cut its peak throughput to
\xo{peakpct/min}--\xo{peakpct/max}\% (one host) and \xo{eks/peakpct/min}--\xo{eks/peakpct/max}\% (cloud) of the
monolith's. Sizing each service to its demand, and heavier business logic, narrowed the gap, but no decomposition
overtook the monolith.
\item Each synchronous call cost a few tenths of a millisecond of transit and of CPU, and a sidecar mesh added
\xo{mesh/perhop/min}--\xo{eks/mesh/perhop/max}\,ms more per call. A \texttt{Server-Timing} header is enough to
measure this price before deciding how finely to split.
\item In decomposed deployments the edge service's CPU quota set the capacity, and CFS throttling made tail latency
erratic before average utilisation reached the limit. Size the edge service rather than the number of services,
and watch its throttled-period counter.
\end{itemize}
