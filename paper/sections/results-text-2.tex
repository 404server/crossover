\subsection{Capacity under an equal budget (RQ2, H2)}
\label{sec:res-capacity}

Figure~\ref{fig:closed} shows closed-loop throughput as a function of the number of concurrent clients, and
Table~\ref{tab:capacity} the measured peaks. The monolith peaked at \xo{peak/browse/A} browse and
\xo{peak/checkout/A} checkout requests per second. The decomposed configurations peaked far lower: B at
\xo{peakpct/browse/B}\% (browse) and \xo{peakpct/checkout/B}\% (checkout) of the monolith's peak, C at
\xo{peakpct/browse/C}\% and \xo{peakpct/checkout/C}\%, and D at \xo{peakpct/browse/D}\% and
\xo{peakpct/checkout/D}\%. No decomposed configuration matched the monolith at any measured concurrency level in
either workload, so the throughput crossover does not exist within the tested range and \textbf{H2 is falsified} by
its first condition. There was no latency crossover either: at no offered rate was a decomposed configuration's
median p99 below the monolith's. The second condition of H2, on $\beta$, is uninformative here, because the fits
for B and C sit at the bound $\beta = 0$ (Table~\ref{tab:usl}).

The cgroup counters show what limits each configuration, and the bottleneck bound of Table~\ref{tab:capacity}
names the same container in every case. At 64 clients the monolith's database container used
\xo{chot/browse/A/64/util}\% of its two-core limit and was throttled in \xo{chot/browse/A/64/thr}\% of its periods,
while the application container used \xo{chot/browse/A/64/apputil}\% of its four cores (checkout:
\xo{chot/checkout/A/64/util}\% and \xo{chot/checkout/A/64/apputil}\%). The monolith is therefore bound by its
database tier. In every decomposed configuration the limit is instead the CPU quota of the edge service:
\texttt{\xo{chot/browse/B/64/appname}} in~B at \xo{chot/browse/B/64/apputil}\% of its single core and
\texttt{\xo{chot/browse/C/64/appname}} in~C at \xo{chot/browse/C/64/apputil}\% of its half core, while their
databases stayed below saturation. The edge service does the most work per request: it parses its callees' JSON
responses, encodes the client's response, and drives every outbound call, so it is the first to exhaust an equal
share of the budget. The rest of the budget then goes unused. At 64 clients the monolith's containers used
\xo{budgetuse/browse/A}\% of the six cores on browse, B's \xo{budgetuse/browse/B}\%, C's \xo{budgetuse/browse/C}\%
and D's \xo{budgetuse/browse/D}\%. The bound assumes that CPU per request does not change with load, so it is an
approximation. It is furthest from the measured peak for the monolith's checkout (\xo{bound/checkout/A} against
\xo{peak/checkout/A} requests per second), whose database needs more CPU per request near saturation than at the
open-loop rate where the bound is evaluated.

Configuration~C is the extreme case. Its throughput was close to its peak with a single client
(\xo{x/browse/C/1} requests per second at $N=1$ against a peak of \xo{peak/browse/C} at $N=\xo{peakn/browse/C}$),
dipped at moderate concurrency and recovered at the highest. With a single client, \texttt{search} already used
\xo{chot/browse/C/1/apputil}\% of its half-core limit and was throttled in \xo{chot/browse/C/1/thr}\% of its CFS
periods; additional clients only queue. D's throughput with one client is lower than C's because of the
sidecars' added latency (\xo{x/browse/D/1} against \xo{x/browse/C/1} requests per second). Its peak was slightly higher (\xo{peak/browse/D} against \xo{peak/browse/C}), but the two intervals overlap
([\xo{peaklo/browse/D}, \xo{peakhi/browse/D}] and [\xo{peaklo/browse/C}, \xo{peakhi/browse/C}]). D's edge application container needed less CPU per request than C's (\xo{hot/browse/D/500/util}\% against
\xo{hot/browse/C/500/util}\% of the same limit at 500 requests per second), consistent with part of its network
processing being charged to the sidecar (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

As a description of the curves, the USL fits the monolith well ($R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}$ of \xo{usl/browse/A/r2adj}
and \xo{usl/checkout/A/r2adj}) and B and D only moderately (\xo{usl/browse/D/r2adj}--\xo{usl/checkout/D/r2adj}).
Their throughput climbs to the quota within a few clients, dips and recovers, a shape the model cannot represent.
For C the fit sits on its bounds and carries no information. For the same reason the capacity figures in this
article are measured peaks, not the fitted $X_{\max}$.
