Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} summarises the three tests. Decomposition costs latency at low load (H1) and the mesh's
cost grows with the number of hops (H3), as predicted. The central hypothesis, that horizontal decomposition eventually
overtakes the monolith (H2), is falsified for an equal CPU budget: the monolith's capacity was \xo{peakfactor/min} to
\xo{peakfactor/max} times that of the decomposed configurations, and no crossover appeared at any load. Allocating the budget in proportion to demand raised their capacity relative to the monolith by a factor of
\xo{alloc/gainmin}--\xo{alloc/gainmax} but did not close the gap. The cloud environment gave the same verdicts,
heavier business logic narrowed the gap to \xo{workprop/peakpct/min}--\xo{workprop/peakpct/max}\% of the monolith's
capacity under proportional allocation, and with autoscalers the monolith sustained several times the decomposed
configurations' load.
