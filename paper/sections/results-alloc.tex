Table~\ref{tab:alloc} compares the equal split with the demand-proportional allocation of
Section~\ref{sec:treatments}. Relative to the monolith measured alongside, moving the budget to where the demand
was raised the capacity of the decomposed configurations by a factor of \xo{alloc/gainmin} to
\xo{alloc/gainmax}. B reached \xo{prop/xmaxpct/browse/B}\% of the monolith's peak on browse and
\xo{prop/xmaxpct/checkout/B}\% on checkout, against \xo{xmaxpct/browse/B}\% and \xo{xmaxpct/checkout/B}\% under
the equal split. C reached \xo{prop/xmaxpct/browse/C}\% and \xo{prop/xmaxpct/checkout/C}\%, against
\xo{xmaxpct/browse/C}\% and \xo{xmaxpct/checkout/C}\%. The monolith's allocation is unchanged, and its peak of
\xo{prop/usl/browse/A/xmax} browse requests per second was close to its \xo{usl/browse/A/xmax} in the first
campaign. No decomposed configuration matched the monolith at any measured concurrency level, so \textbf{H2 is
falsified under this treatment as well}.

The busiest containers explain why. The monolith was still bound by its database. B and C were still bound by
their edge services, now at the limit of a two- to fourfold larger quota. The exception is checkout in~B, where
\texttt{order} was no longer the limit and \texttt{inventory} took its place. Reallocation removed the idle shares
but not the extra work. At 600 browse requests per second, C's application tier needed
\xo{prop/cpums/browse/C/600/app}\,ms of CPU per request against the monolith's \xo{prop/cpums/browse/A/600/app}\,ms.
At that cost even a perfect split of the four application cores serves fewer than half the requests per second
at which the monolith's database saturated.

The treatment also lowered the latency penalty at low load. The p99 ratios to the monolith at the lowest rate fell to
\xo{prop/ratio/checkout/B/25/est}--\xo{prop/ratio/browse/D/50/est}, against
\xo{ratio/browse/B/50/est}--\xo{ratio/checkout/D/25/est} under the equal split. H1 remained supported, as did H3.
Part of the low-load penalty in the first campaign was therefore throttling rather than distribution. With one
client, C's \texttt{search} service was throttled in \xo{chot/browse/C/1/thr}\% of its CFS periods under the equal
split and in \xo{prop/chot/browse/C/1/thr}\% with its proportional share.
